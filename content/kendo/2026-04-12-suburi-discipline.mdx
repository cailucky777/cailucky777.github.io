Sensei reframed warmup suburi today: stop treating it as a metronome and start treating it as a *technical rep*. Each cut is a chance to fix one thing — left grip, right shoulder, stop point.

A few notes I want to keep:

- The kensen should stop where the opponent's men ends, not where my arms run out.
- Haya-suburi is a balance drill. If my heels touch the floor, it's slow enough to feel them.
- Breath: cut on the exhale, reset on the inhale. Not the other way around.
